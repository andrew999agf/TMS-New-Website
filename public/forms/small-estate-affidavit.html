2. Basic eligibility

These questions decide whether a Small Estate Affidavit is even available. Answer honestly — if it isn’t, there are other procedures and we’ll advise you.

Important: a Small Estate Affidavit is only available when the decedent died without a will. If there is a will, a different probate procedure will likely be needed. Please finish the questionnaire anyway — the information is still useful.
Important: a Small Estate Affidavit is not available once an application for administration has been filed or granted. A different procedure will likely be needed. Please finish the questionnaire anyway.
Important: the Small Estate Affidavit is limited to estates of $75,000 or less (not counting the homestead and exempt property). If assets exceed that, a different procedure will likely be needed.
Important: an estate’s assets must exceed its known liabilities for a Small Estate Affidavit to be used. If debts exceed assets, a different procedure may be needed.
Caution: a Small Estate Affidavit can only transfer title to the decedent’s homestead, and only to a surviving spouse or minor child who already had a homestead right in it. Other real estate generally cannot pass this way, and a different procedure may be needed. Please finish the questionnaire — we’ll advise you.

3. Family history

Texas law decides who inherits when there is no will, so we need the full family picture — every marriage and every child, in any relationship, living or deceased.

List every child, even if deceased or estranged. For a deceased child, list that child’s own children (the decedent’s grandchildren) in the description box.

Because the decedent had no children, their parents and siblings may inherit. Please tell us about them.

Please describe the situation in the notes on the last page. An incapacitated heir may require additional steps before filing.

4. What the decedent owned

List everything you know of — bank accounts, vehicles, final paychecks, personal property. Estimates are fine. Account numbers: last four digits only.

Assets with a payable-on-death beneficiary or a joint owner with survivorship rights may pass outside the estate — flag them and we’ll sort out which count.

5. What the decedent owed

Known debts — credit cards, medical bills, loans, taxes. Approximate amounts are fine. If there were none, just continue.

6. Two disinterested witnesses

The affidavit must be sworn to by two people who knew the decedent’s family history but do not inherit anything from the estate — longtime friends, neighbors, in-laws who aren’t heirs.

Not sure who qualifies? Leave what you have and we’ll help you choose proper witnesses.
